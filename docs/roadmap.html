ArchiAI is a browser-based, 3D-first design tool for the first weeks of a residential project: draw the site, shape the mass, and fill it with homes and rooms that already obey the rules an architect would check by hand (daylight, street entry, circulation, adjacency). It is for architects and small practices doing early-stage site and housing design who today stitch together a massing tool, a plan sketcher and a spreadsheet. The engine is deterministic and explainable; AI sits on top as an optional suggestion layer. Everything leaves as clean DXF and IFC, so the work continues in AutoCAD, Revit or ArchiCAD without redrawing.

Evidence base: rule engine fits 26/26 home-quality briefs, mean score 98.5; DXF (AIA layers, mm), IFC4, GLB, OBJ, SVG export shipped.

02Standout components to borrow

Autodesk Forma

Live analysis overlays

Why: sun, daylight and noise read directly on the model turn analysis into a design input, not a report.

Better: tie overlays to our rule engine, so a failing daylight hour highlights the exact room that breaks the hard rule, not just a heatmap.

Arcol

Live metrics panel

Why: GFA, FAR and unit counts updating on every drag is what makes massing feel fast.

Better: metrics go down to homes and rooms (net-to-gross, unit mix, rooms with daylight), and each number links to the geometry it counts.

Modelur

Interactive zoning warnings

Why: seeing a setback or height breach in 3D as you push a face prevents rework.

Better: one warning model for zoning and layout rules, with a one-click fix (trim to envelope, step back top floor) and a plain-language reason.

TestFit

Instant re-solve

Why: change the site and the yield updates in seconds; the tool keeps pace with the conversation.

Better: re-solve respects user locks (pinned rooms, fixed cores) and shows a diff of what moved, rather than a fresh layout.

Snaptrude

Mass → BIM

Why: the massing becomes walls, slabs and spaces without redrawing.

Better: our masses fill with rule-checked homes and export as IFC4 spaces with areas, already a shipped path.

Arcol / Figma

Pinned comments & boards

Why: reviews happen where the model is; boards replace the export-to-slides step.

Better: comments pin to rooms and units and survive re-solve; a board frame is a live view of the model, not a screenshot.

Giraffe

Geolocated site with parcels

Why: starting from the real parcel, terrain and neighbours removes an hour of setup.

Better: keep it light: import a parcel or draw on a map tile, then drop straight into 3D with setbacks applied.

qbiq / Hypar

Three alternatives, side by side

Why: clients choose between options; a single answer invites argument.

Better: alternatives come from the deterministic engine with scores; AI only proposes brief variations and explains trade-offs.

Planner 5D / Hypar

Furniture auto-layout

Why: furnished plans prove a room works and sell the scheme.

Better: furniture feeds back into the rules (bed clearances, door swings), so a furnished room is also a checked room.

05Risks & non-goals

Risks

  • Engine scale. Rules tuned on single homes may not hold across multi-storey plates; the home-quality suite needs a multi-unit counterpart before P3 ships.
  • Browser performance. AO, shadows and analysis on large sites can drop frames; budget per phase and test on mid-range hardware.
  • Zoning data. Rules vary by jurisdiction; start with user-entered rules and presets rather than parsing codes.
  • Crowded market. Forma and TestFit are well funded; our edge is rule-checked housing plus interiors in one model.
  • Editor complexity. The editor page and store are already large; split them before P1 lands more UI.

Non-goals

  • Construction documents, detailing or full BIM authoring.
  • Wind CFD, acoustic simulation, structural design.
  • Photoreal rendering beyond a clean presentation look.
  • Commercial office test-fits (qbiq's market) in the first year.
  • AI-generated geometry that bypasses the rule engine.
